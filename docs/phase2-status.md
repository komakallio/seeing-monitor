## First light, October 4, 2026

The camera ran on the real sky for 49 minutes in three runs (the pointing was lost at each restart), and cirrus ended the night. Times are local.

**What worked.** The detector found 3,000 stars (its limit) in each 30 s frame. The tracker solved 8 frames with 747 to 934 matched stars, a residual of 1.6 to 1.8 arcsec (0.45 pixel), and a plate scale of 3.825 arcsec per pixel (the profile has 3.820). The sky quality gave zero points of 18.65 to 18.96 mag (scatter 0.055 mag, from 430 to 600 stars), a sky of 18.1 to 18.7 mag/arcsec², and a limiting magnitude near 15.2. The fast stream ran at 82 frames per second and closed 42 windows of 20 s, 40 of them with a seeing value. In the calm minutes the median seeing was 1.4 arcsec (r0 7.4 cm, and 1.1 arcsec by the structure function), and in the first 15 minutes it was 3.5 arcsec (1.9 by the structure function), which the motion of the mount explains. These numbers are not calibrated. At 21:38 cirrus took 0.74 mag from the zero point, and by 21:40 it had taken 78% of the flux of Polaris. ## Open design question from the Pi tests

Five restarts of one unit within 10 minutes make systemd give up (`StartLimitAction=none`), and the station then stays down until a person runs `systemctl reset-failed`. A flapping `acquire`, for example from a camera that keeps dropping off the bus, therefore ends in a silent stop with one journal line, because the recovery ladder of `core` ends in a reboot that does nothing without a `reboot_command` and a polkit rule (`--supervisor-actions`). You decide whether the units should end in a reboot (`StartLimitAction=reboot`), or whether the Pi should stay down and alert, and I set it.

## Open item from the Pi 4 preparation

A single exposure on the real camera is slower than the driver's model of it. On the Pi 4 a 1 ms exposure takes 0.29 s for the 20 arcminute watch ROI and 0.48 to 0.53 s for a full bin2 frame (three runs agree within 0.02 s), where the driver predicts 8 ms and 54 ms, because it uses the line model of the video stream for a snapshot. The scheduler waits two periods plus `read_timeout_margin_s` (0.5 s), so the wait is 0.52 s and 0.61 s, and the full frame leaves 0.1 s. The first survey exposure after a start timed out (`no frame within 0.6 s`): the read takes 0.5 s on an idle camera, and the transfer of the frame from `acquire` to `core` and the load of a start probably add the rest. The first recovery step fixed it, and the `camera` component read `degraded` until ten good frames had cleared it (7 minutes at the survey cadence). The stop-gap is `read_timeout_margin_s = 2.0` in `[scheduler.loop]` and in `[services.acquire]`, which the Pi has: the first exposure after its last restart passed. The fix is a snapshot readout model in the profile (about 0.27 s plus 75 µs for each row, from this camera) or a separate margin for snapshots. I have not made it. The runs on the dev machine showed no timeout, and I have not measured a snapshot there.
