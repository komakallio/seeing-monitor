| 10 | Hardware-facing | Done | The ASI SDK binding with a fake SDK, the call watchdog and USB reset, the ASI driver with the recovery ladder, the dew-heater controller with GPIO and sensor interfaces, the power-cycle hook, and the SQM-LE reader. Unverified on real hardware: the SDK structure layouts and control numbers, the libgpiod calls, the USB reset, and the heater constants. The SQM-LE protocol comes from documentation (B5). `seeingmon heater-off`, which the `core` unit runs after it stops, switches every configured heater output to its inactive level. It exits 0 when the outputs are off or no heater is configured, and it exits 1 with one line for each output that it cannot switch off, within a 1 s limit on the GPIO call. Its start-up (the pydantic import) costs 1 to 2 s on the loaded dev machine, so the whole command needs a measurement on a Pi (B2). |
| 11 | Hardware-facing | Done | `seeingmon perf` runs eight cases, each in its own child process (calibration, kernel, fast path, the stream between `acquire` and `core`, a survey frame, the store, memory, and a `core-sim` case). It prints a verdict against each budget with the Pi 4 figures stated as an estimate, and it runs as a smoke test in CI. `docs/performance.md` holds the results on the dev machine (Linux in WSL 2 is the basis, and Windows) and the commands for the Pi 4. The fast path fits with a wide margin: the bin1 kernel takes 23 µs and the fast path 0.35% of a core (estimated 1.8 to 3.8% on a Pi 4). A survey frame takes 1.5 s (estimated 7 to 16 s). The worker peaks at 454 MB (estimated 318 to 590 MB against 550 MB, so marginal), and all processes together peak at 762 MB (estimated 683 to 1,290 MB, so 2 GB of RAM is enough on this evidence). The frame transport between `acquire` and `core` is the risk. At 98 fps, `acquire` takes 6.8% of a dev-machine core (estimated 18 to 40% against its 10% budget), and the receive plus the fast path takes 4.3% (estimated 19 to 35% against 25%, marginal). At 360 fps in bin2, the receive alone would take an estimated 99 to 158%. The services lane is cutting this (batching frames, one poll for each batch, fewer acknowledgements). The perf lane is replacing the `core` stand-in with a measurement of the running system. The Pi 4 measurement stays blocked (B2). |
